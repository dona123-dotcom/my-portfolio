Lesson 3

Big Data Concepts

Understanding Large-Scale Data, Sources, 5Vs & Data Types

Introduction 02 / 16

What is Big Data?

A collection of large and complex data that cannot be efficiently processed using traditional tools (like standard Excel spreadsheets).

Why it Matters

Analyzing Big Data empowers analysts, researchers, and entrepreneurs to make better and faster decisions.

Source 1 03 / 16

Internet-Connected Devices (IoT)

Smart refrigerators, smartwatches, and connected cars continuously collect and generate data regarding location, temperature, behavior, and energy usage.

Example

A smartwatch measures your heart rate, physical activity level, and body temperature and transmits it directly to a mobile application.

Source 2 04 / 16

Social Media Platforms

Daily user activity creates high volumes of data from posts, comments, photos, videos, and likes (Note: these sources may sometimes be unverified).

Example

Sharing an Instagram photo or Facebook post generates data about the exact time, location, reactions, and hashtags used.

Source 3 05 / 16

Financial Transactions

Electronic payments, online banking, and stock market trading contribute to massive streams of transactional data.

Example

An online purchase made with a credit card records details about the amount spent, store name, user ID, and geographic location.

Source 4 06 / 16

Smart Devices & Mobiles

Smartphones, security cameras, and smart home appliances capture continuous data on user interactions, settings, and habits.

Example

Your mobile phone constantly tracks your geographic position and logs the specific places visited and applications opened.

Source 5 07 / 16

Digital Content Consumption

Streaming video, images, and online audio content generate massive metrics regarding views, watch time, and user engagement.

Example

Watching a YouTube video records data on watch duration, user interaction, comments, and share counts.

Sources 6 & 7 08 / 16

Government & Spatial Data

Government Data: Population registers, censuses, and tax data (e.g., regional population income surveys).

Geographic & Spatial Data: Satellites and GPS devices (e.g., Google Maps processing speed and traffic density to optimize routes).

The 5Vs of Big Data 09 / 16
1

Volume

Refers to the massive quantities of data collected and stored. Modern storage capacity lets us accumulate data continuously from millions of sources.

The 5Vs of Big Data 10 / 16
2

Velocity

Refers to the speed at which new data is generated and processed in real time (e.g., instant card payments, live media feeds, connected sensors).

The 5Vs of Big Data 11 / 16
3

Variety

Refers to the diverse formats of data collected—ranging from traditional structured databases to unstructured audio, text, and videos.

The 5Vs of Big Data 12 / 16
4

Veracity

Refers to data reliability and quality. Data may contain errors or biases, making verification crucial before extracting insights.

The 5Vs of Big Data 13 / 16
5

Value

Refers to the actionable usefulness derived from data. Collecting data is only useful if it delivers real benefit to people or organizations.

Types of Big Data 14 / 16

Structured vs. Unstructured

1. Structured Data: Organized in neat tables with rows & columns (e.g., customer banking records, transaction history).

2. Unstructured Data: Has no predefined structure and cannot fit into tables (e.g., text posts, photos, raw video feeds).

Types of Big Data 15 / 16

Semi-Structured Data

A hybrid format combining unstructured content with organizational metadata markers.

Example: Email Messages

The message body contains unstructured text, but it also has structured tags such as Sender, Recipient, Subject, and Date.

Key Takeaway

Dear Students

Always make sure to record data accurately and cite your sources!

Proper citation enhances data reliability and enables easy verification of facts.
